Before and after page: mocks

Companion to the plan: phase-11-before-after.en.html (PL). Five questions, each with variants to pick from. Revised: the reader takes notes while checking the broken page, and the answers (list, markers, fixed page) stay hidden until they finish. Copy inside the mocks is Polish, as in the app. The fictional site is Komunikacja Miejska Wrzosów (KMW), decision 1 of the plan.

The app surface uses the existing tokens. KMW has its own identity: heather purple from the city's name, signal yellow from bus stop signs, and an amber departure board in a monospace face, which is the one thing a reader should remember.

1. Where it lives in the app

1A A third tab in Praktyka, three steps in AU's order

Shown mid-check: the reader has four notes. Steps 2 and 3 say in words that they are locked and why, and have no links yet. The steps are a real sequence, so they are numbered.

2. Checking the broken page, with notes

While checking, nothing on screen points at a problem: no list, no markers, no fixed version, no region names. The notes panel is the same in every variant: one field, a list, "Kopiuj notatki", "Kończę sprawdzanie". Only where it sits changes.

2A The whole window, notes in a drawer from the demo bar

The page gets the real viewport, as in AU. "Notatki" in the demo bar opens a drawer that narrows the page instead of covering it, so it never hides focus. Costs a second copy of the store in the demo document.

Problem

„Rozkłady” rozwija listę linii, gdy najedziesz na nie myszą. Tab przechodzi przez „Rozkłady” do „Bilety”, a lista się nie pokazuje. Czytnik nie wie, że pod pozycją coś jest.

Rozwiązanie

Każda pozycja z podmenu to przycisk z aria-expanded. Enter i spacja otwierają listę, Esc ją zamyka i zwraca fokus na przycisk.

Przed<li class="has-sub"
    onmouseover="open(this)">
  <span>Rozkłady</span>
  <ul class="sub">…</ul>
</li>
Po<li>
  <button aria-expanded="false"
    aria-controls="sub-rozklady">Rozkłady</button>
  <ul id="sub-rozklady" hidden>…</ul>
</li>

5. The look of KMW

Both looks carry the same twenty problems. The question is which one teaches better. Both shown broken; 5B also shown fixed, with the same layout, so the difference sits where the fixes are.

5A A municipal portal from around 2012

Gradients, Verdana at 11 pixels, three columns at a fixed 1000 pixels, a ticker. Familiar to anyone who audits public sector sites, and it makes problem 20 obvious. The risk: readers file accessibility under "old sites".
