Mocks: mobile menu

The header below sm (640px). Today the eight links wrap onto two rows under the wordmark, which costs about 150px of every phone screen before the content starts. Pick a variant: A, B, C or D. Each one is shown at 340px, closed and open, in light and dark. Colours, type and sizes come from src/styles.css. Desktop stays as it is.

Defaults I chose where your answers were still open: the breakpoint stays at 640px, and the theme toggle moves into the menu in A, B and C (it doesn't fit in the bar next to a menu button without dropping its text label).

Today, for reference

A. "Menu" button, list opens under the bar (recommended)

  • A <button aria-expanded aria-controls> labelled "Menu" shows a full-width list under the bar and pushes the page down. This is the disclosure pattern the site already teaches in /praktyka/wzorce/rozwin-zwin.
  • No focus trap and no inert. Tab moves from the button straight into the links and then on to the page. Escape closes the list and returns focus to the button. Navigating to another page closes it.
  • The label stays "Menu" in both states, so the accessible name doesn't change. Only aria-expanded and the chevron do.
  • Bar height when closed: 61px instead of about 150px. At 400% zoom the list scrolls with the page, so nothing is cut off.
  • Code: one small client component with one useState, plus an e2e test for keyboard and Escape.

B. Full-screen menu in a dialog

  • "Menu" opens a native <dialog> with showModal(). The page behind becomes inert, focus goes to the first link, and Escape or "Zamknij" closes it and returns focus to "Menu".
  • Large type: links are 56px high and easier to hit. This is the strongest "app" feel of the four.
  • Trade-off: the reader loses sight of the page, and it is a modal for something that doesn't need to be one. Opening and closing must not animate under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Code: a bit more than A (dialog ref, closing on route change), and a focus-return test.

C. Bottom bar for the thumb, the rest under "Więcej"

  • A fixed bar at the bottom of the screen with the three most-used sections and "Więcej". "Więcej" opens a sheet with the remaining four links and the theme toggle. "Start" is the wordmark at the top.
  • Easiest to reach with one hand. The top bar shrinks to just the wordmark.
  • Trade-offs: two navigations instead of one, and someone has to decide which sections count as main. It collides with the cookie banner, which also sits at the bottom, and with scroll-padding-bottom (2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured). At 400% zoom a fixed bar takes a large part of the screen, so it would have to unfix itself in landscape and short viewports.
  • Code: the most of the four. The sheet is a dialog like B, plus the bar, plus layout fixes for the banner.

D. No button: one row that scrolls sideways

  • Keeps today's idea that every link is visible without an extra tap, but in one row of 44px instead of three. The current page is scrolled into view on load.
  • The fade on the right edge is the only hint that there is more. Readers who don't swipe sideways may never find "Słownik". With a keyboard it works: Tab moves through the links and the row scrolls along.
  • Trade-off: hidden content with a weak signal. A "1 z 8" style counter helps a little but adds noise. 1.4.10 Reflow allows this, but it isn't great.
  • Code: CSS only plus one scrollIntoView. No state.
