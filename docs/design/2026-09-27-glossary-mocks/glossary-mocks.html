Glossary mocks: phase 2

Two decisions. First, how a term looks where it first appears in a criterion page (A, B, C). Second, how the glossary page at /slownik is laid out (D, E). Pick one from each group. All five use the variant A tokens already in the app. Sample criterion 4.1.2. Definitions quoted "w normie" are the real text of the authorized Polish translation of WCAG 2.1. The header also shows the new draft badge from the plan.

Terms are marked by the author in Markdown, [nazwa](slownik:nazwa), not matched automatically, because Polish inflection makes automatic matching unreliable. Only the first occurrence on a page gets the treatment below; later ones are plain text.

A: Definition opens under the paragraph

The term is a button with a dotted underline and a small question mark. Activating it opens the definition as a block right under the paragraph, pushing the text down. Nothing floats, so nothing covers focus (2.4.11) and zoom and reflow work as for any text. A disclosure button with aria-expanded. Shown open.

B: Gloss in the margin

No interaction at all. On a wide screen the definition sits in the margin next to the paragraph that uses the term, like a sidenote in a textbook. On a narrow screen it drops under the paragraph. The marker is a number because it pairs a term with its note in reading order. Always visible, so nothing is hidden behind a click, but the page gets denser and the margin competes with the "Na tej stronie" rail, which would have to move above the sections.

C: Term links to the glossary, a small button shows a preview

The term itself is an ordinary link to its glossary entry, so it works without JavaScript and opens in a new page like any link. Next to it, a small round button opens a toggletip with the short definition. Esc and the close button dismiss it, focus stays on the button. The fastest to read for sighted mouse users; the preview floats over text, so it must never cover the focused element, and it is the most code of the three. The 24px button relies on the inline-target exception in 2.5.5. Shown open.

D: Glossary page, A to Z

One page, alphabetical, with a letter index. Letters without terms are plain text, not dead links. Each entry is a heading, the plain explanation first, the normative wording next to it, and the criteria where the term matters. Works like a dictionary: good when you already have a word to look up.

E: Glossary page, grouped by topic

The same entries grouped by what you are working on, with a field that filters the list as you type. Works like a textbook index: better for someone learning, who does not yet know which word to look up. Each term still has its own anchor, so links from criterion pages land on it. The filter is a plain text field with a live count; without JavaScript the full list shows.
